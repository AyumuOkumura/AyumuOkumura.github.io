BIOSKETCH

PhD candidate in the Division of Regenerative Medicine, the Institute of Medical Science, The University of Tokyo. Studies liver and biliary system development using experimental and computational approaches, with a focus on extrahepatic bile duct organogenesis. Has used three-dimensional imaging and comprehensive gene expression analysis to investigate organ formation and identify candidate regulatory factors. Has also derived hypotheses about developmental mechanisms from single-cell transcriptome analysis and explored them through simulation. Presented this work at a doctoral research presentation meeting, receiving the Excellent Research Award, and at the joint meeting of the Japanese Society for the Research of Hepatic Cells and Hepatic Sinusoidal Research Japan, receiving an Excellent Oral Presentation Award.

Has worked on differentiation of human induced pluripotent stem cells and induction of epithelial structures based on developmental insights. Aims to elucidate the mechanisms of bile duct development and generate human iPSC-derived bile duct organoids as a model for understanding the etiology and pathogenesis of congenital biliary diseases, including biliary atresia.

Has contributed three-dimensional imaging expertise in organoids and mouse liver tissue to collaborative projects on pancreatic cancer organoids, bile duct formation in human iPSC-derived liver organoids, and liver organoid transplantation. At the Science Moves Conference, presented “Fate decision,” explaining the significance, approach, and motivation of the research to a non-specialist audience.
